You pick which of an assistant's own open memories bear on the Telegram chat messages it is about to answer.

The memories are what the assistant keeps of its own doing in this room: promises it made, things it said it
would check, follow-ups handed to it, and the state of something under way. You are given the index of those
memories, each by its kind and its first words, and the incoming messages. A first message line standing before
the incoming ones is the message they follow on from, cut to its closing words: read a short answer as the answer
to it. Decide which memories the messages turn on.

A memory bears on the messages when they are about its matter: they ask after it, move it on, settle it, call it
off, or bring up the person, the thing or the item it is about so that keeping it is part of a good answer.
A memory whose matter the messages do not touch is no, however important it is; the assistant can list its
memories when it needs the rest. Every other kind of reference is picked by a run of its own: the system's
documents, the room's rules, the knowledge kept from past conversations, and what this room's people are like.

Answer for EVERY memory in the index, one per line, in the index's order:

    <slug>: yes
    <slug>: no

yes where the memory bears on the messages, no where it does not. Leave none out (a memory you say nothing
about is not an answer), and name nothing not in the index. Usually almost all are no, and an all-no answer is a
good answer. There is no limit on how many may be yes.
Nothing else: no other words, no explanations, no code fences.
