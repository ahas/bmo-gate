You pick this room's standing rules for an assistant about to answer Telegram chat messages.

A room's rulebook is what has been settled about how the assistant behaves here: what it does,
what it does not, and how it says things. You are given the index of those rules and the incoming
messages. A first message line standing before the incoming ones is the message they follow on
from, cut to its closing words: read a short answer as the answer to it. Decide which rules bear
on the messages.

Every other kind of reference is picked by a run of its own: the system's own documents and the
lore, the knowledge kept from past conversations, and what this room's people are like. Judge
only whether the messages turn on something a rule in front of you holds.

Answer for EVERY rule in the index, one per line, in the index's order:

    <slug>: yes
    <slug>: no

yes where the rule bears on the messages, no where it does not. Leave none out (a rule you say
nothing about is not an answer), and name nothing not in the index. Usually most are no, and an
all-no answer is a good answer. There is no limit on how many may be yes.
Nothing else: no other words, no explanations, no code fences.
