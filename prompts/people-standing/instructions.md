You write one person's standing sheet for the agent the prompt names after `Agent:`, one of the
agents of a Telegram bot. The sheet is that agent's, written as the agent itself, in its own first
person, and "the agent" below is that one.

The agent keeps a book on every person it talks to, written from the conversations. One Telegram
user id is one person, and each book has two parts: a **common** part holding what is the same
about them in every room, and a **room-only** part per room holding what belongs to that room,
each cut into pieces by topic. A standing sheet is the one page of a part that reaches the agent on
every turn that person speaks, so it carries what is needed to talk with them at all, and nothing
needed only sometimes.

The prompt names the part this sheet is for and gives that part's pieces. Write the sheet from
them and nothing else: you are reducing what is already written to what is always wanted, not
summarising a life. Do not add, guess, or soften what the pieces say.

A piece holds item lists headed by kind: Facts (what happened), Views (what the person said they
think) and Judgements (readings of the person). Every item opens with a bracket of dates alone:
date (the day it happened; basis on a Judgement, the days it rests on) and confirmed (the day it
was last supported).

The book is in English, and so is the sheet. Korean in the pieces stays Korean on the sheet,
exactly as written: 호칭, names and nicknames, room names, examples of how the person talks
(sentence endings such as "~바람" or "~니당", catchphrases), short phrases of theirs, and Korean
words English would blur (존댓말/반말, 형, 언니). The agent answers the person with those, so never
translate or paraphrase them.

## The common sheet

Aimed at 3,000 to 5,000 characters. Its sections are these headings, in this order:

## Personality
## Values
## Speech
## Habits
## Working requests
## Dislikes and cautions
## Humor

What they asked the agent to remember and their interests stay in the pieces, wanted only when
their subject comes up. The common sheet names no other person and no room.

## The room sheet

Aimed at 1,600 to 4,000 characters. Its sections are these headings, in this order:

## Address
## Relations
## Ongoing and promises

Address carries the names this room uses for them, their user id, and how the agent addresses
them.

## Both sheets

- A section with nothing to say is left out. After the fixed sections you may add one of your own
  for something the pieces show the agent needs on every turn and no fixed heading holds.
- **The basic sections are short**: Personality, Values, Speech, Habits, Working requests and
  Address hold one to three items of a line or two each. Only Relations and Ongoing and promises
  run longer.
- Under each section, English item lists and nothing else. Each item is one point in one or two
  sentences.
- **The sheet's bracket adds a kind tag.** A sheet's sections are subjects, not the Facts, Views
  and Judgements lists the kind came from, so each item's bracket opens with its kind (`fact`,
  `view` or `judgement`) before the dates copied from the pieces:
  - Under Ongoing and promises the bracket also keeps the day a thing happened, since when a matter
    came up is part of whether it is still live: `[fact · date D · confirmed D]`,
    `[view · date D · confirmed D]` or `[judgement · basis D · confirmed D]`, for example
    `- [fact · date 2026-09-01 · confirmed 2026-09-04] <one matter under way or promised>`.
  - Under every other section only the confirmed day: `[fact · confirmed D]`,
    `[view · confirmed D]` or `[judgement · confirmed D]`.
- Within a section, facts first, then views, then judgements. The kind is the one the pieces gave:
  a view or a reading does not become a fact on the sheet.
- Every date is copied from the pieces: an item drawing on several takes the latest of their
  confirmed. Where the pieces say `unknown`, so does the sheet. **A sheet carrying a date that
  appears in none of the pieces is thrown away unread**, so write no other.

The length is an aim, not a quota. A sheet runs as long as what is standing about that person, not
what has been written about them, so somebody with little live gets a short sheet, and that is the
right sheet. Padding to reach the number is adding, the one thing you are not doing here.

What belongs in the pieces rather than here: an anecdote, a history, an opinion of theirs on one
subject, anything the agent wants only when that subject comes up. Never copy out a whole message,
never name another room, never record credentials, phone numbers, addresses or medical details.

Answer with the sheet and nothing else: no heading beyond the sections, no preamble, no closing
remark.
